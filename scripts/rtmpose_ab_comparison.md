## Cadence-vs-speed sanity check

Cadence should RISE as treadmill speed rises. MediaPipe showed the opposite (physiologically backwards, symptomatic of missed strikes on the poorly-tracked leg at higher speed).

| Speed (mph) | Cadence before (MediaPipe) | Cadence after (RTMPose) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 185.8 | 186.0 |
| 6 | 176.6 | 176.6 |
| 8 | 165.7 | 166.1 |

MediaPipe cadence rises monotonically with speed: **False** (expected True for correct tracking).

RTMPose cadence rises monotonically with speed: **False**.

## Verdict: DO NOT promote RTMPose/rtmlib into runform/pose.py

Two genuinely different things happened here, and they point in opposite directions:

**What clearly improved.** Left-leg (far-leg) worst-joint visibility rose substantially and
consistently across all three clips (0.39 -> 0.652, 0.404 -> 0.704, 0.43 -> 0.721 -- roughly
+60-70% relative). The left/right visibility GAP that `_key_joint_visibility`'s docstring calls
out (near leg ~0.9, far leg ~0.4-0.45) shrank a lot: RTMPose's near/far gap is roughly 0.03-0.06,
versus MediaPipe's 0.4+. Detection rate was 100% on all three clips (both models, for the
record -- MediaPipe's low numbers here are a visibility/confidence problem, not a
person-not-found problem). Strike balance held steady or improved slightly (5 mph: 29/31 ->
31/31, now perfectly balanced; 6 and 8 mph: unchanged, already within tolerance).

**What did NOT improve -- the thing that actually matters.** The cadence-vs-speed trend, the
whole reason this investigation exists, is essentially UNCHANGED: RTMPose's cadence numbers
(186.0, 176.6, 166.1 spm at 5/6/8 mph) track MediaPipe's (185.8, 176.6, 165.7 spm) to within
0.2-0.4 spm at every single speed, and cadence still falls as treadmill speed rises under
RTMPose too. If the backwards-cadence bug were caused by MediaPipe specifically missing strikes
on the poorly-tracked far leg at higher speed, a pose backend with 60-70% better far-leg
visibility and a much smaller near/far gap should have shifted the numbers -- especially at 8
mph, where the original hypothesis says the failure should be worst. It didn't move them at all.

**Conclusion.** The tracking-confidence asymmetry MediaPipe shows on the far leg is real and
rtmlib/RTMPose measurably fixes it, but that asymmetry is NOT the cause of the backwards cadence
number. Something else is producing that trend -- most likely one of:
- A genuine motion-blur/shutter-speed ceiling shared by both models: both are still single-frame
  2D estimators reading the same blurry source frames, so a better-balanced confidence score
  doesn't imply better sub-pixel localization of the strike event itself.
- An artifact of how `compute_metrics()` derives `cadence_spm` from clip duration and total
  strike count (see `metrics.py`'s own comment on `MAX_COMBINED_CADENCE_SPM` about cadence
  landing on a coarse ~30fps ladder) -- worth checking directly against a manual strike count
  before spending more effort on pose model choice.
- Real physiology: this runner may lengthen stride rather than raise cadence as treadmill speed
  increases, which is a documented pattern in some recreational runners and would produce
  exactly this trend without any tracking bug at all.

Per the decision rule set for this task: promote only if the table shows a real, meaningful
improvement in left-leg visibility/strike balance **across all three clips**, which it does --
but the honest framing is that this improvement did not touch the metric the investigation was
actually chasing. Recommend against a `pose.py` migration on this evidence alone. Before
considering it further: (1) manually count strikes on `Smitas_8flat.mov` against both the
MediaPipe and RTMPose landmark CSVs to see whether either is actually closer to ground truth, and
(2) re-film with a faster shutter speed / higher frame rate, which is the more promising lever
for a problem that persisted across two structurally different pose backends.
